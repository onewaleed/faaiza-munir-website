About Faaiza Munir

British author writing character-led cosy crime, complicated family hierarchies, and mysteries driven by humour, warmth, and relentless sisterly interrogation.

Faaiza Munir was born in Scotland to Pakistani parents and spent her formative years navigating two distinct cultures with one constant companion: an insatiable appetite for murder mysteries.

Growing up in a loud, affectionate, and fiercely opinionated household, she quickly observed that the investigative methods used by Golden Age detectives were remarkably similar to the interrogations conducted by aunties at British-Pakistani family weddings: polite on the surface, forensic in detail, and utterly inescapable.

After studying literature and theatre in Edinburgh and London, Faaiza worked in cultural communications and script development. However, her enduring fascination remained with classic British whodunits. She adored the intricate puzzle-box mechanics of Agatha Christie, Dorothy L. Sayers, and Richard Osman, but found herself asking a simple question: What would happen if the drawing-room murder took place not in an isolated manor house, but in the middle of a chaotic, gossip-fuelled family gathering where everyone has a motive and nobody wants their samosas to get cold?

“In my family, nobody solves a problem quietly. We solve it over six pots of chai, three conflicting theories, and someone inevitably pointing out that the prime suspect was poorly dressed anyway.”

The result is her debut novel, Arranged Murder, the first book in The Asghar Family Mysteries. Centred around four fiercely distinct sisters—Amina, Zara, Nadia, and Shireen—the series celebrates sisterhood, generational friction, cultural nuance, and the sheer absurdity of human secrets.

When she isn’t writing at her London desk surrounded by multi-coloured index cards and an alarming quantity of tea, Faaiza can be found prowling independent bookshops, attending fringe theatre productions, or visiting family back in Scotland.
